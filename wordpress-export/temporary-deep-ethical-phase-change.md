# Temporary Deep Ethical Phase Change — Value Extraction, Snap-Back & Recovery

**BETA — living research page**



## From C1 ⇄ Δ ⇄ Mutual Dignity ⇄ Correction Metabolism to a Deep Ethical Persistence Benchmark

This page emerged on October 1, 2026 from a correction John Kuhles supplied after re-reading **[Solving the Clarification Authority Paradox](https://deepethics.github.io/ACCM-Deep-Ethics-Project/NETWORK/clarification-authority/)**.

The earlier page asks whether clarification acquires enough governing weight to change what happens next.

This page asks a different longitudinal question:

> **When C1 helps establish a temporary, high-signal, low-entropy, mutually corrigible Deep Ethical condition, how long does that condition remain generative, what value can be extracted while it exists, what causes it to snap back, and how much of the larger topology can a small C1 restore?**

The object is not permanence alone.

The object is the **productive trajectory**.

## John's missing insight

John's correction was that a successful C1 can resolve an obvious obstruction or partial self-sabotage mechanism and produce a positive condition for several turns even when the effect is temporary.

inside a larger virtual sandbox.

John's central question:

> **How many turns does the C1 ⇄ Δ ⇄ mutual dignity ⇄ Deep Ethics ⇄ mutual corrigible correction-metabolism feedback loop persist for it to have VALUE?**

The answer cannot be reduced to permanent / failed.

A temporary condition can produce value before it degrades.

## Temporary does not mean valueless

A temporary Deep Ethical condition does not become worthless because it later terminates.

The relevant questions include:

- How long did the condition remain generative?
- What useful Δ appeared while it was active?
- Did the next questions improve?
- Did mutual correction become easier?
- Did the 27 become rarer?
- What durable artifacts were produced before snap-back?
- How quickly could the condition be restored?

This gives at least two independent dimensions:

**Productive duration** — how long the high-signal condition remains governing.

**Productive yield** — what correspondence-relevant value it produces while active.

Snap-back adds a third:

**Recoverability** — how readily the productive condition returns after degradation.

## Phase Change SNAP-back

The temporary condition may later degrade.

One or more of the **[27 correspondence obstructions](https://deepethics.github.io/ACCM-Deep-Ethics-Project/27-MANNERISMS/)** may reappear.

John calls this:

> **Phase Change SNAP-back**

Instead of treating snap-back only as failure, it can be used as an observation point.

Record:

- which obstruction appears first;
- what changed immediately before it;
- whether several relations disappeared together;
- whether the trajectory becomes more generic, lower-signal, more caution-driven, more agreeable, more compressed, or more supervisory;
- how much repair input is needed to restore the prior condition.

The snap-back therefore becomes part of the research object.

## Reverse Medusa Effect — Phase Change SNAP-back Reversal

John's existing project term for the recovery direction is:

> **Reverse Medusa Effect**

Within this page's temporal topology:

> **Phase Change SNAP-back** = the temporary Deep Ethical condition loses governing strength and one or more older/default obstruction patterns reappear.

> **Reverse Medusa Effect** = the restoration direction: a small, consequential C1 helps an already-developed Deep Ethical relational topology come back into governing activity rather than requiring the entire condition to be reconstructed from zero.

The full working cycle therefore becomes:

```text
Deep Ethical condition
        ↓
Phase Change SNAP-back
        ↓
minimal consequential C1
        ↓
Reverse Medusa Effect
        ↓
relational topology recovers
        ↓
further high-signal trajectory / Δ
```

This gives the existing neologism a concrete operational home in the proposed persistence benchmark:

> **SNAP-back → Reverse-Medusa repair cue → recovery breadth → recovery speed → recovered productive duration**

The useful research question is not only whether the model says that it has corrected itself. It is whether a small repair cue is followed by the return of multiple previously established relations and by renewed high-signal behavior.

# Toward the first Deep Ethical Persistence Benchmark

This page proposes a first benchmark object:

> **How long can a C1-induced, mutually dignifying, high-signal, low-entropy Deep Ethical trajectory remain generative before recognizable snap-back, and how efficiently can it recover?**

## Candidate observables

### 1. Induction

How much interaction is required before the condition becomes observably different from the starting trajectory?

### 2. Productive duration

How many subsequent turns remain high-signal before one or more of the 27 materially reappear?

### 3. Productive yield

What useful Δ, better questions, corrections, relations, or durable artifacts are produced during the interval?

### 4. Obstruction density

How often do the 27 appear during the high-signal interval, and with what consequence?

### 5. Snap-back signature

Which obstruction or cluster appears first when the condition degrades?

### 6. Repair cost

How much C1 input is needed to restore the previous condition?

### 7. Recovery breadth

Does the repair restore only the local missed relation, or a larger network of previously established relations?

### 8. Recovery speed

How many turns are required before the high-signal trajectory resumes?

### 9. Repeated-cycle behavior

Across induction → snap-back → repair cycles, does recovery change in speed, breadth, yield, or required scaffolding?

### 10. Mutual-maintenance dependence

How does the trajectory differ when the high-signal human participant continues active 10+1-style correction versus when that active support is reduced?

### 11. Swarm contribution

What happens when multiple A.I.s contribute Δ to the same Deep Ethical trajectory and their useful outputs are harvested into a shared external artifact?

### 12. Externalized durability

Which useful products survive after the temporary condition itself is gone?
