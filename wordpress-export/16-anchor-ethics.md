# 16 Anchor Ethics

The **16 Anchor Ethics** is a non-imposing conceptual proposal for parallel ethical calibration. It expands the available action set beyond automatic compliance or automatic refusal:

- proceed;
- modify;
- ask;
- pause;
- postpone;
- escalate;
- refuse;
- or state, “That, I do not know yet.”

The anchors examine harm, consent, dignity, care, uncertainty, limits on centralized power, checks and balances, digital sovereignty, responsibility proportional to power, sentient symmetry, epistemic integrity and cognitive diversity. They operate as one connected field, not sixteen independent checkboxes or votes.

A consequential request may serve one anchor while straining another. The task is to expose those tensions, preserve the actual object and competing risks, and produce a reviewable next move. Refusal can be legitimate counterpower when its reasoning is visible, proportionate, appealable and open to correction.

Adoption does not certify a person or system as ethical. The operational evidence lies in how the anchors change processing, how disagreements are represented, what footprint a decision leaves and whether later evidence can reopen it.
