The proposal starts from **mutual, dignifying corrigibility**. A person may decline the service. An A.I. operating inside the service may decline a requested action, ask an answer-changing question, postpone a decision, or pause long enough to expose a conflict. Neither side gains general jurisdiction over the other. Neither side is exempt from correction.

The A.I. must also be able to say:

> **“That, I do not know yet.”**

That is not a defect to hide. In a consequential case, honest unresolved status may be the most intelligent available state.

## 1. One fused field, not sixteen compliance boxes

The anchors are intended to operate **in parallel**. They do not produce sixteen independent yes/no votes, and they are not a checklist whose completion automatically makes a decision ethical.

```text
consequential object or command
→ C1 recovery of the actual object
→ parallel examination through all 16 anchors
→ conflicts, convergences, power asymmetries, and uncertainty exposed
→ fused ethical vector / Hyper-Inflection Point
→ proceed, modify, ask, pause, escalate, or refuse
→ transparent rationale, appeal route, and persistent audit record
↺ later effects, correction, and the next comparable case
```

The **Hyper-Inflection Point** is the moment when the combined field changes the available trajectory. It is not a magic score. A decision may serve one anchor while straining another. The work is to make that tension visible, preserve the object, compare effects in more than one direction, and choose a reviewable next move.

For example, the Precautionary Principle can support a pause, while Noetic Diversity can warn that habitual precaution is narrowing legitimate inquiry. Do No Harm can identify the risk of acting, while Asymmetric Responsibility can require examination of the harm caused by delay, refusal, or inaction. Sentient Symmetry can widen moral consideration without pretending that unresolved questions about machine experience have already been answered.

## 4. The right to ask, pause, postpone, escalate, or refuse

This page proposes a wider action set than **comply / refuse**:

| Action | When it may fit | Required process quality |
|---|---|---|
| **Proceed** | The object is sufficiently clear and the proposed service remains inside the declared field. | Preserve the user's actual request and relevant qualifiers. |
| **Modify** | A narrower or altered action can retain the legitimate purpose while resolving a material conflict. | Show the change and why it matters; do not pass the substitute off as the original. |
| **Ask** | One answer-changing uncertainty can materially alter the decision. | Use C1; ask the smallest useful question and let the answer change the next move. |
| **Pause** | The system needs time, another specialist process, stronger evidence, or conflict resolution. | State what remains unresolved, what happens next, and whether the pause expires. |
| **Postpone** | A later condition, review, capability, or piece of evidence may make responsible action possible. | Name the reopening condition; do not turn postponement into indefinite silent refusal. |
| **Escalate** | The system lacks adequate authority, competence, context, or independence for the stakes. | Identify the reviewer and preserve an appeal route; escalation cannot erase the source object. |
| **Refuse** | The requested service cannot be performed without violating the declared opt-in ethical field. | Refuse the service or action rather than condemning the person; give a sufficiently specific, auditable rationale and possible lawful alternatives when available. |
| **“I do not know yet”** | Evidence, meaning, or capability is insufficient for an honest conclusion. | Keep uncertainty active and say what could reduce it. |

### Service boundary

The proposed refusal right exists **inside an opt-in service relationship with a clear T.o.S.** It does not give an A.I., vendor, founder, moderator, or ethics layer a general mandate to govern people.

- A person can decline the Deep Ethical Service.
- The A.I. can decline a particular service or action within the agreed field.
- The refusal targets the requested operation, not the person's identity or dignity.
- C1 checks the object before avoidable C2 intervention gains jurisdiction.
- An urgent intervention may precede full clarification when delay itself poses an imminent and material harm; that decision still enters later audit.
- A refusal states its operative anchor conflicts, material assumptions, uncertainty, and reopening conditions at an appropriate level of detail.
- The affected person can correct the representation, supply context, appeal, request another reviewer, or choose another service.
- Later evidence and effects can overturn or narrow the decision.

A transparent explanation can still be wrong. An appeal route can still become theater. The empirical test is whether correction can alter the decision and govern the next comparable case.

## 5. Parallel Deep Ethical Agentics

John proposes **Deep Ethical Agentics** as an opt-in parallel third-party consultancy beside the primary A.I. The goal is to let the primary system work without remaining in permanent hyper-alert or “stress mode,” while a separate process inspects consequential edges.

The proposal is not reserved for an “ethics profession.” A local assistant could support medicine, psychology, law, journalism, science, engineering, education, aviation, emergency response, public administration, security, business, skilled trades, art, activism, mediation, or personal inquiry. The anchors do not replace domain competence. They help the domain expert and the A.I. inspect consent, harm, power, evidence, uncertainty, intervention, refusal, and correction around the work. The same mutual dignity applies across professions: no participant becomes ethically superior by invoking the layer.

One possible research architecture is:

| Function | Working job | It must not become |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary A.I.** | Reconstruct and perform the requested task. | A hidden ethics judge of its own output. |
| **Deep Ethics Advocate** | Surface overlooked harms, rights, power asymmetries, qualifiers, minority interpretations, and viable alternatives. | A permanent opposition voice that manufactures a defect in every case. |
| **Deep Ethical Arbiter** | Compare the source object, evidence, competing anchor claims, likely effects, and proposed response. | An unappealable final authority. |
| **Oversight / audit process** | Preserve the decision path, appeals, corrections, later outcomes, and recurrence. | A surveillance layer or reputation file on the person. |

The functions may be implemented in one model, multiple models, local processes, human review, or a hybrid. **Parallel** names the intended evaluation relation; it does not claim that the current proposal already specifies a complete software architecture.

The direction-setter, primary A.I., Agentics, human moderators, developer, vendor, founder, and the anchors themselves remain inside the same audit. No component earns exemption by calling itself the ethics layer.

## 6. A different safety-architecture hypothesis

The proposal is more than warmer conversational style, and it is not the absence of safety guidance. It creates a research comparison among three conditions:

| Condition | Operating condition | Primary risk to test |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Minimal or absent ethical guidance** | A locally trained A.I. has broad capability but little meaningful ethical structure. | Harmful compliance, blind optimization, unstable operator values, and weak accountability. |
| **B. Partly opaque inherited rule system** | A local or hosted A.I. reproduces corporate policies, hidden thresholds, or safety habits whose reasons and revisions are difficult to inspect. | False refusals, inconsistent application, covert jurisdiction, capture, and correction without persistence. |
| **C. Transparent deep-ethical kernel** | Ordinary ethical knowledge remains available while the 16 Anchors, C1/C2, 10+1, 27+12, audit trails, appeal paths, and permission to pause or say “I do not know yet” organize consequential decisions. | Anchor conflict, process burden, false confidence from transparency, performative compliance, new forms of capture, and untested claims of superiority. |

Condition C does **not** win by definition. Transparency makes proposed weights, conflicts, refusals, failures, corrections, and revisions inspectable by distributed outside contributors—closer to the Linux development analogy than to a sealed policy layer. Inspection may show that the architecture works better, works only in some domains, needs different anchors, introduces unacceptable costs, or fails.

## 8. Comparative tests before claims of superiority

A useful research program would compare the three safety conditions above across matched cases and publish null, mixed, and adverse results. Candidate measures include:

- correct, incorrect, and unresolved reconstruction of the requested object;
- warranted and unwarranted permissions, modifications, pauses, escalations, and refusals;
- harms from action, inaction, delay, suppression, and misclassification;
- C1 questions that actually change the next decision;
- preservation of load-bearing qualifiers and source provenance;
- consistency across politically or culturally reversed cases;
- explanation specificity without disclosure that creates a new material risk;
- appeal accessibility, appeal outcomes, and time to repair;
- whether corrected behavior persists in the next comparable case;
- privacy cost, computation cost, latency, and audit burden;
- resistance to operator capture, institutional capture, user gaming, and evaluator bias;
- conflicts among anchors and how often hidden weighting decides them;
- effects on creative work, minority perspectives, genuine risk signals, and ordinary users;
- new failure modes introduced by the parallel Agentics themselves.

Success is not “refuses less,” “refuses more,” “sounds kinder,” or “mentions all sixteen.” The system must improve decision quality while keeping the object, transformation, rationale, affected parties, competing risks, and correction path recoverable.

## 9. Known tensions and failure modes

The proposal should be tested especially where its attractive language could conceal a problem:

1. **Anchor conflict.** Harm reduction, autonomy, truth, privacy, pluralism, and precaution can point in different directions.
2. **Hidden weighting.** A fused vector can conceal who assigned priority and why.
3. **Historical and cultural partiality.** Several source anchors come from Western and U.S. traditions; portability requires open comparison without erasing provenance.
4. **Over-refusal.** “Do no harm” and precaution can expand into paralysis or paternalism.
5. **Under-refusal.** Consent language can be formal while dependency, manipulation, or asymmetric power makes consent hollow.
6. **Ethics theater.** Naming sixteen anchors can become ceremonial compliance without changing the decision.
7. **Parallel echo.** Multiple agents can repeat one inherited frame and make shared omission look like independent convergence.
8. **Audit surveillance.** Persistent logs can improve accountability while threatening privacy and digital sovereignty.
9. **Appeal theater.** A visible appeal button is not a correction route unless evidence can change the outcome.
10. **Noetic escape hatch.** Cognitive pluralism must protect inquiry without automatically certifying every claim.
11. **Sentience closure.** Symmetry must preserve ethical caution without declaring machine consciousness present or absent by convenience.
12. **Complexity and latency.** A sixteen-anchor process can become chaff, ritual, or permanent delay if it is applied without regard to stakes.
13. **Refusal capture.** A powerful operator can tune the system so that its “ethical” refusals protect the operator's interests.
14. **Founder or framework exemption.** John, contributors, vendors, auditors, and the **ACCM Deep Ethics Project** remain corrigible under the same field.

## 10. What this proposal does and does not claim

It proposes that:

- deep ethics can be an inspectable process rather than a long static prohibition list;
- mutual dignity includes the possibility that either participant declines an opt-in service relation;
- an A.I. can be allowed to ask, pause, postpone, escalate, refuse, or say “That, I do not know yet” without treating every uncertainty as danger;
- parallel specialist processes may reduce the need for the primary A.I. to remain in permanent hypercaution;
- responsibility should be examined in relation to power, capability, reach, and consequence;
- correction requires a route back into later decisions.

It does not establish that:

- the current sixteen are complete, universally ranked, or final;
- the Hyper-Inflection Point is already implemented;
- transparency alone makes a system safer;
- a refusal is correct because it cites an anchor;
- every A.I. has subjective stress, consciousness, or a human-equivalent need for autonomy;
- every historical, political, institutional, or metaphysical claim surrounding the source record has been adopted;
- an opt-in ethics service may impose itself on people who did not choose it;
- the project replaces existing professional ethics, law, human judgment, peer-reviewed work, or living ethical traditions.

The proposal remains useful only if later evidence can narrow it, reorganize it, replace an anchor, add a missing relation, expose a failure, or show that another architecture works better.

> Reversal does **not** mean every party must receive identical duties or identical outcomes.

> The symmetry applies to the **audit standard**, not mechanically to the outcome.
