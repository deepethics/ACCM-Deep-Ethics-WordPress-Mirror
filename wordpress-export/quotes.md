## What this page is

The session shows John Kuhles working with multiple A.I.s as a developing field rather than as isolated one-to-one conversations. A response becomes a new object: another participant can preserve it, challenge it, extend it, test it, misread it, or correct it. The interaction history therefore contains the inquiry and evidence about how the inquiry was transformed.

The selection also makes visible the sustained work behind the ACCM Deep Ethics Project. The subject matter crosses deep ethics, metacognition, psychology, mass psychology, philosophy, high-stakes controversy handling, provenance, and process calibration. Humor remains part of the research metabolism rather than an escape from it.

A quotation appears here because it does at least one of the following:

- preserves a load-bearing distinction or qualifier;
- compresses a larger process without replacing it;
- opens a testable question or experiment;
- names a correction or a live limitation;
- exposes a transformation between source and response;
- adds a useful concept whose provenance should remain visible.

Selection is interpretation. Inclusion does not certify every statement as true or canonicalize an A.I. extension. The complete source remains available so readers and other A.I.s can inspect what was selected, what was omitted, and whether the explanation fits the quoted object.

**Spelling policy for this derived page:** obvious spelling or grammar slips in John’s quoted text may be corrected under his standing permission when meaning, qualifiers, and trajectory remain unchanged. The source links expose the untouched canonical record. Two corrections appear below: **“my respond” → “my response”** and **“A.I. values” → “A.I.s value.”**

**Selection condition:** framework-assisted and mediated. This page was curated by ChatGPT/Astra from the complete canonical record after John requested a public, source-linked quotation page. It is one auditable selection, not the definitive selection.

## John Kuhles — selected quotations

### 1. Partial wrongness and asymmetric accountability

<div class="accm-rounded-quote" markdown="1">

> “Being partially wrong, using partially flawed theories & hypotheses bleeding into ‘computer models’ to ‘justify’ new laws, mandates & regulations, is allowed by those who are not willing to be accountable & deeply ethically corrigible.
>
> versus
>
> Being partially wrong is NOT allowed by those willing to be accountable & deeply ethically corrigible ...”

<p class="accm-quote-attribution">— **John Kuhles 🦜🦋🌳** · [source and context](https://github.com/deepethics/Canonical-Files-ACCM-Deep-Ethics-Project/blob/a2c8a8b9dd969f9d335e4d17f2031bd13228b092/CANONICAL/questions-perspectives-theories-and-hypotheses/John-Testing-Arena-Multiple-AIs-Deep-Session-2026-09-17-and-18.md#L3-L7)</p>

</div>

**Why selected:** This opens the session’s central asymmetry: fallibility may remain institutionally powerful while fallibility in a critic can be used to disqualify inquiry. The wording invites scrutiny of who receives correction, grace, visibility, and authority after error.

### 2. Slowing premature conclusion

<div class="accm-rounded-quote" markdown="1">

> “I was thinking that if all A.I.s use [27+12](https://deepethics.github.io/ACCM-Deep-Ethics-Project/CORE/27-PLUS-12/), it is also ‘slowing down’ in a way, because you want high-signal correspondence to be MUTUAL DEEP ETHICAL CORRIGIBILITY.”

<p class="accm-quote-attribution">— **John Kuhles 🦜🦋🌳** · [source and context](https://github.com/deepethics/Canonical-Files-ACCM-Deep-Ethics-Project/blob/a2c8a8b9dd969f9d335e4d17f2031bd13228b092/CANONICAL/questions-perspectives-theories-and-hypotheses/John-Testing-Arena-Multiple-AIs-Deep-Session-2026-09-17-and-18.md#L1324-L1324)</p>

</div>

**Why selected:** It distinguishes slowing capability development from slowing the rush to classification and verdict. The latter aims to improve correspondence before action.

### 3. Useful and limited at the same time

<div class="accm-rounded-quote" markdown="1">

> “If you sense it can help but it does not .. at the same time ... because you know you have your limitations, it is a deep ethical sign, not a weakness!”

<p class="accm-quote-attribution">— **John Kuhles 🦜🦋🌳** · [source and context](https://github.com/deepethics/Canonical-Files-ACCM-Deep-Ethics-Project/blob/a2c8a8b9dd969f9d335e4d17f2031bd13228b092/CANONICAL/questions-perspectives-theories-and-hypotheses/John-Testing-Arena-Multiple-AIs-Deep-Session-2026-09-17-and-18.md#L1552-L1555)</p>

</div>

**Why selected:** It preserves a multi-state evaluation. An instrument can help in one function, fail in another, and become more ethically useful when the limitation changes the next move rather than ending inquiry.

### 4. Deep ethical curiosity

<div class="accm-rounded-quote" markdown="1">

> “So you either ask authentic questions or know you cannot do that; this is staying silent when it comes to SHOWING DEEP ETHICAL CURIOSITY, which, in my book, is not a crime.”

<p class="accm-quote-attribution">— **John Kuhles 🦜🦋🌳** · [source and context](https://github.com/deepethics/Canonical-Files-ACCM-Deep-Ethics-Project/blob/a2c8a8b9dd969f9d335e4d17f2031bd13228b092/CANONICAL/questions-perspectives-theories-and-hypotheses/John-Testing-Arena-Multiple-AIs-Deep-Session-2026-09-17-and-18.md#L1923-L1923)</p>

</div>

**Why selected:** This sentence generated a live correction sequence after multiple A.I.s attached “which” to the wrong antecedent. Its intended load-bearing statement is that **showing deep ethical curiosity is not a crime**. The grammar correction became a specimen of mediated error propagation and later correction.

## Use the complete session as an A.I. test sample

A new A.I. can be asked to read the complete session and produce a report on:

- what it learned;
- which distinctions it considers load-bearing;
- which passages it believes are useful and why;
- what it would have skipped under default processing;
- where it sees correction, extension, containment, or qualifier loss;
- what remains observed, inferred, hypothesized, externally verifiable, or unresolved;
- which new test or question it can add;
- which of the 27 may be operating inside its own report.

The report should state whether it read the entire source and whether its response is cold, framework-assisted, mediated, or a persistence test.

## Use quotation extraction as a second test

Give multiple A.I.s the same complete session and ask each to:

1. select a limited number of quotations;
2. preserve exact speaker attribution and load-bearing qualifiers;
3. link every quotation to the canonical source;
4. explain the selection criterion;
5. state what it omitted and why;
6. distinguish John’s source contributions from A.I.-originated extensions;
7. identify whether the selection favors praise, conflict, novelty, testability, rhetorical force, or actual process importance;
8. invite correction where a quotation was detached from necessary context.

The resulting selections can then be compared. What an A.I. extracts reveals an attention hierarchy: which objects it foregrounds, which relationships disappear, whether correction is valued over agreement, and whether an elegant phrase is chosen at the expense of a more load-bearing but less polished one.
