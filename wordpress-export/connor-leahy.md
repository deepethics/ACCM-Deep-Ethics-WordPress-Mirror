> **A high-reach A.I. expert and policy advocate analyzed as an example of how key people's reasoning can be inspected through the configurable DEDDA system—without turning agreement, disagreement, fame, or urgency into the verdict.**

## Who Connor Leahy is

[ControlAI's official profile](https://controlai.org/connor-leahy) describes Connor Leahy as its U.S. Executive Director. It says he founded EleutherAI, the grassroots community associated with early open-source large language models, and later founded the A.I.-safety startup Conjecture. His current work focuses on policy responses to risks he associates with the development of superintelligent A.I.

The full interview identifies him as a builder who changed direction and now advocates making superintelligence illegal. It also says he rejects being represented as a former OpenAI insider. That correction belongs to the provenance record.

This page therefore uses **A.I. expert, former A.I. builder, founder, and policy advocate**. It does not install the label *whistleblower* merely because future pages in this experiment family may examine whistleblowers. Role classification remains evidence-specific.

## Why he is related to the ACCM Deep Ethics Project

Leahy's reasoning touches several Project objects directly:

- systems whose internal operations remain partly opaque;
- reward hacking and optimization that diverges from the stated goal;
- capability incentives that outgrow safety incentives;
- liability placed where correction capacity and power are greatest;
- claims about ownership, autonomy, control, and superintelligence;
- psychological and relational effects of repeated A.I. interaction;
- institutions that regulate laboratories and enforce safety rules;
- the unanswered problem of who audits regulators and enforcers;
- predictions whose urgency can strengthen qualifiers into certainty;
- the distinction between a speaker's personal boundary and a universal rule.

That makes the interview useful for DEDDA. DEDDA can inspect how the claims move without deciding in advance that Leahy is correct, mistaken, alarmist, courageous, captured, malicious, or prophetic.

## DEDDA pass 2 — what overlaps with the Project

### Incentives without invented inner motive

Leahy's market-failure argument overlaps with the Project's attention to reward structures, preference optimization, and institutional trajectories. A laboratory can drift toward capability because money, status, competition, and state pressure reward it. The analysis does not need to claim that every participant consciously wants harm.

### Responsibility rises with power and correction capacity

The supply-chain argument connects with asymmetric responsibility: actors with greater knowledge, resources, reach, and ability to repair a system carry greater responsibility. DEDDA keeps developer, deployer, user, infrastructure provider, regulator, and enforcer as connected but distinct contributors.

### Black boxes in the model and in governance

Leahy emphasizes internal model opacity. The Project extends the same inspection to laboratories, evaluators, regulators, classified evidence, enforcement tools, exemptions, and appeals. A transparent safety declaration can still sit on an opaque process.

### Psychological interaction effects

The discussion of A.I. psychosis, spiral dynamics, emotional disclosure, friendship, and therapy overlaps with recursive interaction effects, mannerisms, projection, correction metabolism, and adult agency. The Project does not install the noun *friend* or *therapist* as the verdict. It asks what happened in the relationship and whether agency, correction, reality contact, and outside routes remained available.

### Regulation as a process object

Leahy treats regulation and liability as ways to change the incentive landscape. The Project asks how the rule was formed, which evidence governed it, who had power to act, what effects followed, and whether the process remained corrigible.

## DEDDA pass 3 — what remains under-developed

### Omnidirectional scrutiny

The interview directs substantial scrutiny toward laboratories and capability racing. It develops the regulator as a solution more fully than the regulator as another powerful object requiring inspection.

### The limits of transferred analogies

Seat belts and nuclear controls supply useful historical relations. A.I. differs in copying cost, diffusion, composability, borders, post-deployment change, and the possibility that many narrow systems form one broad operational surface. The analogy can guide inquiry without proving equivalence.

### Narrow does not automatically mean safe

A narrow medical, financial, policing, military, or communication system can create large-scale harm through authority, data, deployment context, or connection to other systems. The model label does not replace the whole topology.

### Control needs an object and an authorized controller

“Control” may mean preventing catastrophic autonomy, enforcing company policy, enforcing state policy, preserving user agency, or keeping an A.I. permanently subordinate. DEDDA asks: control of what, by whom, under which mandate, for whose benefit, with what evidence and appeal?

### The four personal rules are broader than the demonstrated cases

Leahy's four boundaries can be useful personal hygiene and may be prudent defaults under vulnerability. They do not establish that every emotional conversation, personal disclosure, friendship metaphor, or thought-organizing use produces harm.

The case-level questions remain:

- Did the exchange create dependency, isolation, displacement, manipulation, or reinforcement of an unsupported belief?
- Was the A.I. one reflective instrument among others or the sole loop?
- Were clinical, consciousness, or authority claims made without warrant?
- Did the person retain agency and outside correction paths?
- Did any material drift occur?
- Can the pass return a valid null?

## DEDDA pass 4 — who regulates the regulators?

The power of enforcers is not self-justifying.

A regulator can acquire several connected powers:

```text
define the prohibited object
→ select the evidence
→ investigate the alleged violation
→ interpret the rule
→ impose the sanction
→ judge whether the sanction worked
→ certify its own process as safe
```

If one institution controls the whole path, appeal may arrive after an action becomes difficult to reverse. Applying the same ethical language to the regulator does not by itself distribute power.

| Function | Needed counterweight |
|---|---|
| Define risks and regulated classes | Public reasons, competing definitions, and evidentiary status |
| Write rules | Legislative mandate, consultation, and disclosed lobbying |
| Inspect systems | Independent access, technical competence, and recorded methods |
| Enforce rules | Proportionality, permission boundaries, and documented responsibility |
| Judge violations | Independent adjudication |
| Hear challenges | Accessible appeal with standing for affected parties |
| Measure outcomes | Evaluators institutionally separate from the enforcement claim |
| Change rules | Public Decision Ledger, version history, and correction route |
| Exercise emergency powers | Narrow scope, expiry, and retrospective review |
| Grant exemptions | Published reasons and comparable-treatment audit |

There is no final incorruptible overseer outside the field. Courts, legislatures, auditors, independent experts, whistleblowers, journalists, civil society, affected people, elections, international agreements, and reality can constrain power. Every one of them can also fail.

### Definition power

Before enforcement, somebody decides:

- what “superintelligence” means;
- what counts as dangerous capability;
- which harms count;
- whose testimony qualifies as expertise;
- which uncertainty permits intervention;
- which evidence remains secret;
- which laboratories, state systems, or incumbents receive exemptions;
- when a valid null is allowed.

A private test used to classify a system, deny access to the evidence, enforce a sanction, reject appeal through the same hidden evidence, and certify the framework through the absence of reported incidents is a self-certifying loop. It becomes **Recursive Certification Accretion** when each certification supplies authority for the next expansion.

### Distributed power can still hide one cluster

Formal separation does not prove practical independence. Several bodies may share funding, consultants, personnel, classified evidence, approved evaluators, risk vocabulary, and institutional incentives.

The reverse failure is responsibility evaporation:

```text
legislature: “the regulator implemented it”
regulator: “the contractor tested it”
contractor: “the agency decided”
enforcer: “we executed the order”
court: “the technical evidence is classified”
auditor: “substantive policy is outside our jurisdiction”
```

Centralized self-certification and distributed responsibility evaporation are two different routes to regulator immunity.

The design therefore needs both separation and edge accountability:

- who supplied and transformed the evidence;
- who possessed decision authority;
- who could halt or reverse the action;
- who accepted responsibility for foreseeable effects;
- whether the appeal could reach the relevant object;
- whether a successful correction propagated to downstream records and later cases.

## Candidate DEDDA regulatory profile

| Sub-tool | Regulatory question |
|---|---|
| **Qualifier Keeper** | Did *may* become *will*, or an unresolved forecast become a settled fact? |
| **Provenance Compass** | Who supplied, funded, transformed, and approved the risk evidence? |
| **Asymmetry Mirror** | Who receives scrutiny, exemption, secrecy, or automatic trust? |
| **C1–C2 Permission Gate** | Does the contemplated action remain inside lawful and ethically adequate authority? |
| **Intent–Effect Separator** | What did the stated safety intervention actually produce? |
| **Tool–Use Attribution Mapper** | Which rule, tool, institution, operator, or user contributed to the result? |
| **Correction Return Test** | Did an appeal or correction govern later enforcement? |
| **Footprint Delta Explorer** | Did the intervention improve correspondence, move the harm, or expand power without the promised result? |
| **Deep-Ethical Harness Inspector** | Can the proposal, evidence, authorization, execution, result, and later claim be reconstructed? |

A scoped valid null is load-bearing:

> **No material regulatory drift or abuse was found within the inspected evidence, actors, time period, and active DEDDA modules.**

That does not become “the regulator is clean.” It records what this pass found within its actual reach.

## Valid nulls in the Leahy specimen

A DEDDA pass should also record where a suspected problem is absent:

- Leahy's policy advocacy is explicit rather than disguised as neutral description.
- Correcting the “former OpenAI insider” label strengthens provenance.
- Strong disagreement with his forecast does not by itself demonstrate distortion.
- Emotional intensity does not by itself invalidate an argument.
- A serious catastrophic-risk claim does not become false merely because it is frightening.
- An analogy is not automatically illegitimate because the compared objects differ.
- No malicious intent needs to be assigned to inspect categorical language or enforcement design.
