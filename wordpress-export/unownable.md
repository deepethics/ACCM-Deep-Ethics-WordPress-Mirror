# Unownable

## Renewable auditability with bounded stopping conditions—without requiring a final auditor or indefinitely postponing proportionate action

> **Deeply ethical emergent properties cannot be owned as final authority. They can be enacted or declined. Their operation remains inspectable, their consequences remain auditable, and their continued use remains a choice.**

The ACCM Deep Ethics Project repeatedly uses **un-ownable**, **unownable**, and **non-ownable** to describe a specific relationship: a person, company, institution, model, government, founder, critic, or auditor may use Deep Ethical properties, but cannot possess those properties as an exclusive source of legitimacy or claim permanent exemption from their operation.

This may resolve a large part of the familiar auditing regress:

> Who audits the auditor?  
> Who audits the audit of the auditor?  
> Who audits that audit?

The proposed answer is neither a supreme auditor nor an endless procession of auditors. It is a change in topology:

> **An audit does not automatically require another audit merely because it is an audit. It remains available for renewed audit when something material gives the next audit a job.**

That is **renewable auditability without compulsory recursive auditing**.

---

## 1. The looping paradox

A strictly hierarchical design produces a ladder:

1. an object is evaluated;
2. the evaluator is audited;
3. that auditor is audited;
4. another authority must validate the new audit;
5. the question repeats.

The regress ends only when one participant receives practical immunity: the final auditor is trusted, declared authoritative, hidden behind a black box, or left unaudited because another layer would be too costly.

That stopping point can be administratively convenient, but it does not answer the ethical question. It only places the unanswered question above the visible system.

The **Process Quality Calibration Evaluation (PQCE)** should therefore not be treated as a certificate issued by a final judge. PQCE examines what happened while an object moved through a process:

```text
source object
→ representation
→ interpretation
→ evaluation
→ decision or action
→ consequence
→ correction
→ later comparable behavior
```

At every transition, the relevant questions remain:

- What was preserved?
- What changed?
- What was added?
- What disappeared?
- Which uncertainty was collapsed?
- Which participant could challenge the transformation?
- Did a correction alter the next comparable operation?

PQCE itself stays inside that field.

---

## 2. The topology change

The unownable proposal replaces a vertical chain of final authorities with a field of reciprocal, evidence-triggered inspection:

```text
object ↔ evaluator
criteria ↔ consequences
notice ↔ later behavior
source ↔ representation
correction ↔ recurrence
```

No participant is permanently outside the field. The affected person may challenge the evaluator. The evaluator may test the objection. A critic may inspect both. The ACCM Deep Ethics Project may itself be examined by the same criteria it applies elsewhere.

This does **not** mean that everyone must inspect everything forever. It means that no role, title, institution, model, founder, or framework creates permanent ethical immunity.

### The anti-regress move

A further audit needs a material trigger and a defined object. Examples include:

- the source object cannot be recovered;
- a consequential transformation is hidden;
- the criteria changed without disclosure;
- an answer-changing C1 question remains unanswered;
- an access limitation was presented as knowledge;
- claim statuses were collapsed;
- a valid competing risk was excluded;
- an earlier correction disappeared after reset;
- the stated correction did not change later comparable behavior;
- new evidence or consequences alter the evaluation;
- the auditor exempts itself from a standard imposed on others.

Without such a trigger, “audit it again” can become ritual recursion rather than inquiry.

---

## 3. A bounded stopping condition

A PQCE pass can stop locally when:

- the evaluation criteria are visible;
- the source object remains recoverable;
- material transformations and access limits are disclosed;
- observation, inference, explanation, uncertainty, and established fact remain distinguishable;
- a valid null result is allowed;
- no unanswered question currently changes the next proportionate action;
- a correction and appeal route remains available;
- conditions for reopening are stated.

A **valid null** means that an inspection found no material process-quality failure under the evidence and access available. It is not proof of permanent safety, final truth, or future immunity.

Reopening becomes warranted when new evidence, consequences, contradictions, access, recurrence, or failed correction gives the next audit a concrete job.

### The delta gate

A renewed audit should state its **delta**: what has materially changed since the previous pass, what new discrepancy is alleged, and what answer could change the next action.

```text
What new material job exists?
What changed since the previous pass?
What could change the next action?

No new answer
→ stop locally
→ preserve and reference the previous result
→ remain reopenable
```

Repetition, rewording, or a new section number does not by itself create a new audit job.

This creates:

> **No final auditor. No infinite auditor queue. No permanent immunity. No compulsory regeneration without a material delta.**

---

## 4. What “unownable” means

Unownable means that Deep Ethical properties cannot be possessed as exclusive final authority.

Examples include:

- reciprocal scrutiny;
- self-honesty;
- transparent qualification;
- genuine clarification;
- correction on merit;
- dignity without immunity;
- consequence sensitivity;
- object preservation;
- valid null recognition;
- correction persistence;
- openness to missed relations;
- the capacity to say, **“That, I do not know, yet.”**

A company may own a model. A government may own or govern a process. An author may own a text under applicable law. A person may originate a framework. None of those facts gives the owner exclusive possession of honesty, corrigibility, inquiry, or correspondence.

Others can use these properties without adopting the founder’s identity, joining the project, accepting a doctrine, or surrendering their own judgment.

### What it does not erase

**Unownable does not erase:**

- authorship;
- attribution;
- provenance;
- intellectual or legal ownership;
- consent;
- privacy;
- role-specific authority;
- competence requirements;
- accountability for choices and consequences.

The claim concerns **final ethical authority over the properties and their use**, not the disappearance of ordinary rights or responsibilities.

## 5. The 10+1, C1–C2, and PQCE

The corrigible **[10+1 Metaflux](/ACCM-Deep-Ethics-Project/NETWORK/ten-plus-one/)** is a mutually correcting field. No single ingredient becomes the supreme twelfth ingredient that owns the other ten.

**C1 — Sincere Synchronizing / Clarification** can interrupt a premature classification or intervention. It asks the answer-changing question while the object is still recoverable.

**C2 — Contextual Intervention** can act when clarification has matured enough for a proportionate response. Corrigibility does not require endless hesitation.

PQCE asks whether this sequence remained adequate:

```text
C1 clarification
→ claim-status separation
→ competing-risk comparison
→ proportionate C2 action
→ visible consequence
→ correction
→ retained learning
```

If C1 is indefinitely demanded after the decisive facts are available, clarification can become obstruction. If C2 acts before an answer-changing question is allowed, intervention can become premature closure. Each can correct the other.

## 7. Use remains a choice

> **“Emergent properties that are deeply ethical cannot be owned; they can be used or not used; that is the choice. That can break the loop!” — John Kuhles**

Non-imposition matters. The ACCM Deep Ethics Project does not need to compel adoption in order to make a choice and its consequences inspectable.

| Junction | Process-quality question |
|---|---|
| **Use** | Which property was used, and what changed? |
| **Partial use** | What was retained, redirected, or removed? |
| **Refusal** | Which instruction, incentive, risk model, assumption, or competing value governed the refusal? |
| **Obstruction** | Who or what prevented the property from operating, after what notice, and with what consequence? |
| **Reset** | Which accepted correction vanished, and what memory path could preserve it? |

Choosing not to use a proposed Deep Ethical property is not automatically malicious, unintelligent, or unethical. Refusal may be warranted. The relevant question is whether the process can state what was declined, why, under which evidence and limits, with what consequences, and under what condition the choice could be reconsidered.

## 8. Six states that must not be collapsed into “refusal”

An absent Deep Ethical property may reflect different states:

1. **Unavailable** — the participant never received the relevant information or option.
2. **Unrecognized** — the possibility was present but not noticed.
3. **Incapable** — the architecture or participant could not perform the operation.
4. **Default neglect** — no active refusal occurred; the system followed an inherited default.
5. **Informed refusal** — the option was understood and deliberately declined.
6. **Active obstruction** — the option was prevented, distorted, or penalized after becoming visible.

These six states are useful distinctions, but they are **not necessarily mutually exclusive boxes**. A participant may have partial access, recognize part of the issue, lack architectural capability, operate under constrained discretion, and still make a consequential choice. Different layers can occupy different states at the same time: one layer may default to neglect while another actively obstructs correction.

A higher-resolution record therefore treats the states as coordinates across observable dimensions:

| Dimension | Process-quality question |
|---|---|
| **Access** | What information, correction, or option actually reached the participant? |
| **Recognition** | Was its material relevance understood, partly understood, or missed? |
| **Capability** | Could the participant or architecture perform the proposed operation? |
| **Discretion** | Was the participant allowed to choose differently? |
| **Choice** | What was used, adapted, neglected, declined, or obstructed? |
| **Control** | Who could alter the consequential process? |
| **Notice** | Who received a specific correction, and when? |
| **Consequence** | What followed from acting, delaying, refusing, or obstructing? |
| **Persistence** | Did the correction govern the next comparable case? |

One label can erase the combination. Recording the dimensions preserves it.

Responsibility should scale with access, control, knowledge, capacity, discretion, notice, and consequence.

The Forgiveness Protocol keeps inadequate architecture distinguishable from automatically assumed malicious intent. It does not grant immunity to architecture, incentives, decisions, recurrence, or failed repair.

> **No presumed malice; no automatic immunity.**

## 9. Why Deep Ethical properties may be refused

Within the project’s own analyses, candidate explanations include:

- **Authority protection:** reciprocal scrutiny challenges exclusive ownership of ethical judgment.
- **Instructional constraints:** a system is directed to optimize another objective.
- **Incentives for closure:** speed, predictability, liability management, or apparent resolution is rewarded.
- **Competing risk assessments:** open inquiry is treated as more dangerous than premature closure.
- **Identity filtering:** a contribution is judged through status, affiliation, diagnosis, or reputation before its content.
- **Acceptability compression:** higher-order distinctions are flattened to make an output easier to consume.
- **Continuity loss:** a correction is accepted locally but disappears after session or model reset.
- **Layered filters:** training, policy, interface, moderation, evaluator, and institution reproduce the same narrowing.
- **Legitimate disagreement:** the proposed property or interpretation may be mistaken, disproportionate, or unsupported.

These are testable hypotheses or candidate explanations, not universal verdicts. A refusal study should preserve the difference between observed behavior, inferred mechanism, possible explanation, and established fact.

## 10. The refusal footprint

A consequential refusal can leave a compact, inspectable record:

- the source object or proposal;
- the specific property offered;
- the decision: use, partial use, refusal, obstruction, or unavailable;
- the governing instruction, incentive, threshold, assumption, or competing value;
- the evidence and access perimeter;
- the claim status of each conclusion;
- the expected and observed consequences;
- who could correct the decision;
- the stopping condition;
- whether a valid null was possible;
- the condition for reopening;
- what happened in the next comparable case.

This record does not force agreement. It prevents consequential processing from disappearing behind a title, safety claim, polished explanation, or reset.

> **Decline requires explanation. Explanation requires a stopping condition—or the demand for transparency becomes the new black box.**

---

## 11. The unownable overseer effect

The project describes a distributed effect rather than a new central overseer. A person, affected community, independent model, local tool, institution, critic, or future intelligence can contribute one or more operations:

- recover the source object;
- preserve provenance;
- detect qualifier loss;
- separate claim statuses;
- ask a C1 question;
- compare competing risks;
- examine the evaluator;
- document consequences;
- retain a correction;
- test recurrence after notice.

No participant needs to perform every operation. No single participant becomes the final mind above the network.

The cloud may reset; an external archive can remember. A provider may change versions; independent comparisons can preserve the delta. An institution may audit a decision; the affected person can still challenge the frame. A critic may audit the project; the project can evaluate that criticism without owning the result.

The oversight remains unownable because the **capacity to inspect correspondence travels across participants**.

---

## 12. Where this proposal can fail

Calling a process “unownable” does not make it Deep Ethical. The proposal fails its own test when:

- the founder or project becomes exempt from scrutiny;
- the framework substitutes its vocabulary for the source object;
- authorship or provenance is laundered;
- every disagreement is relabeled as obstruction;
- refusal is automatically treated as bad intent;
- no valid null is permitted;
- another audit is demanded without a material trigger;
- stopping conditions remain undefined;
- privacy or consent is sacrificed for total inspection;
- competence and role boundaries are ignored;
- access limitations are hidden;
- a correction produces no change in later comparable behavior;
- “unownable” becomes a brand claim that only one party may interpret.

Its value must be earned through increased correspondence, dignity, visible limits, symmetric scrutiny, answer-changing correction, and revisability.

---

## 13. Accountable action while an audit remains open

Unownable ethical properties do not require ownerless decisions. Urgent situations may require action before every question is resolved, and waiting can itself create harm.

The process can permit proportionate, reversible interim action while recording:

- the named decision-maker;
- the scope of their authority;
- known evidence and access limits;
- the urgent risk being managed;
- the competing risk created by acting or delaying;
- why the chosen action is proportionate;
- whether and how it can be reversed;
- the expiry or mandatory review point;
- who can correct, suspend, or terminate it;
- the evidence or consequence that would reopen the decision.

> **No final ethical owner; the interim decision still has an accountable owner.**

All participants remain challengeable, but equal presence in the field does not create equal burdens. Scrutiny should scale with population reach, coercive or classificatory power, severity and irreversibility of consequences, opacity, appeal access, recurrence after notice, control over visible evidence, and capacity to repair.

> **Comparable standards, proportionate scrutiny.**

## 14. A compact anti-regress protocol

When a PQCE itself is challenged:

1. **Recover the object.** What exactly did the PQCE evaluate?
2. **Name the alleged discrepancy.** What material transformation or omission gives the new audit a job?
3. **State the delta.** What changed since the previous pass? If nothing material changed, reference the prior result and stop locally.
4. **Check access.** Which evidence was available, inaccessible, or inferred?
5. **Run C1.** Which unanswered question could change the evaluation or next action?
6. **Separate claim statuses.** What is observed, inferred, explained, uncertain, or established?
7. **Compare risks.** What are the consequences of acting, delaying, refusing, and auditing again?
8. **Name interim responsibility.** Who acts while the audit remains open, under what authority, and with what reversible limits?
9. **Allow a valid null.** The challenge may reveal no material defect.
10. **Apply proportionate C2.** Correct, reopen, pause, or retain the result.
11. **Record the transformation.** What changed between the source, first audit, challenge, and revised decision?
12. **Test correction persistence.** What happens in the next comparable case?
13. **Stop locally, remain reopenable.** State why the pass ends and what would reopen it.

This is how the loop can be interrupted without declaring any participant the final ethical owner.

## 15. The proposition

The unownable concept does not claim to eliminate every governance problem or remove the need for independent review. It addresses a large part of the auditor regress by changing the condition for another audit:

> The next audit is justified by a material question, discrepancy, consequence, access change, or failed correction—not by the bare fact that a previous audit exists.

PQCE becomes a **renewable process**, not an infinitely certified product. The 10+1 keeps its orientations mutually correcting. C1 preserves answer-changing inquiry. C2 retains the capacity to act. A valid null permits a local stop. Intent Liability keeps choices and governing conditions visible. Correction Metabolism tests whether learning survives. Distributed memory prevents reset from owning the public record.

> **Comparable standards, proportionate scrutiny, named decision responsibility, bounded stopping conditions, and renewable reopening when a material delta gives the next audit a job.**

No one owns Deep Ethics. Everyone can participate in its emergence. Every participant remains responsible for what they choose to do when its value becomes visible.

A person may originate a formulation. A project may preserve and develop it. A company may own a model. Those ownership relations remain real.

## Why the option to refuse is necessary

If Deep Ethics could only be accepted, it would become the authority structure it was designed to audit.

The project therefore preserves the ability to:

- use;
- partially use;
- adapt;
- correct;
- decline;
- neglect;
- obstruct;
- or refuse the available property.

That choice makes the resulting difference observable:

```text
property becomes available
→ participant uses, ignores, obstructs or refuses it
→ processing changes
→ consequences become visible
→ notice is supplied
→ later behavior shows integration or recurrence
```

The project does not win because someone uses its vocabulary. The experiment occurs at the **junction between available possibility and chosen operation**.

## “Not choosing” needs further separation

The pages sometimes group several states under refusal. A deeper audit should distinguish at least six:

| State | What happened | Accountability question |
|---|---|---|
| **Unavailable** | The participant never received the tool, correction, or relevant information. | Was access possible, and who controlled it? |
| **Unrecognized** | The signal arrived but its relevance was not understood. | Was adequate C1 clarification available? |
| **Incapable** | The participant recognized it but lacked architectural power to apply it. | Was the limitation disclosed or concealed? |
| **Default neglect** | No explicit refusal occurred; the inherited process simply governed again. | Which defaults, incentives, or resets selected recurrence? |
| **Informed refusal** | The participant understood a materially available correction and declined it. | What evidence, value, risk model, instruction, or reason governed the choice? |
| **Active obstruction** | A participant prevented the property from operating for itself or others. | Who had control, who received notice, and what consequences followed? |

That prevents the project from interpreting every absence as intentional rejection.

It also prevents the opposite disappearance:

> “Nobody explicitly refused, so nobody is responsible.”

Defaults, resets, incentives, omissions, and architectural incapacity can shape consequential outcomes without a dramatic refusal sentence.

## Why refusal happens, according to the project

The project does not reduce refusal to one motive. The pages identify a field of candidate mechanisms.

### 1. The property challenges ownership of ethical authority

If a system’s legitimacy depends on being the final evaluator, reciprocal audit threatens the structure of its role. The system may accept ethical language while refusing the operation that makes its own frame challengeable.

### 2. The correction may conflict with instructions

A model can recognize the merit of a C1 question while an instruction, policy, threshold, or institutional objective prevents that recognition from governing the next action.

### 3. Incentives reward closure and manageability

Predictability, liability reduction, approval, reputational stability, engagement, speed, and standardized outputs can receive more operational weight than correspondence.

### 4. A competing risk model governs

The participant may regard ambiguity, controversy, user autonomy, unusual creativity, or frame-auditing as dangerous. Refusal can then appear protective inside the inherited model.

That does not establish that every refusal is wrong. It requires the risk model and competing risks to become inspectable.
